GEO604: Water & Planetary Habitability

Semester: Fall 2026 · Credits: 3 · Location: ESS 322 · Time: Tuesdays 1:30–3:30 pm
Instructor: Junjie Dong · Email: junjie.dong@stonybrook.edu · Office Hours: By appointment

Description

Water is inseparable from life as we know it and from the physical, chemical, and dynamical processes that connects planetary interiors, surfaces, atmospheres, and orbits. This seminar explores the role of water in planetary habitability by surveying relevant literature from the fields of geophysics, geochemistry, atmospheric science, and planetary science. Topics include the origin and evolution of water on Earth; its coupling to climate, tides, and interior convection; the divergent outcomes on Venus and Mars; and the existence of subsurface oceans on icy moons and water-covered exoplanets. The aim is to understand, and perhaps to provoke new ways of thinking about, how water enables, mediates, and sometimes limits planetary habitability.

Assessment

Paper Discussion (formative, 100%): 2–3 students lead each week. They choose 2–3 papers and each student presents 1 paper. They write 2 prompts or questions per paper, and post them to the course site by Friday. Everyone comes having responded in writing to at least 1 prompt per paper. After the presentation in class, share your responses, discuss, push back, and build on each other's ideas. By end of day on the day of class: submit final written responses to all prompts or questions (2–3 sentences each), plus any questions the reading or discussion left open. Discussion leaders meet briefly with the instructor beforehand to select papers.
